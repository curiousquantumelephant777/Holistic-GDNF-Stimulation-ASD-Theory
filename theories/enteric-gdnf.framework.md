## 🦠 The Enteric Axis and Interstitial Cells of Cajal (ICCs) in Neurodevelopmental Disorders: A GDNF-Centric Framework

### Insight
Gastrointestinal comorbidities are frequently documented in Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). This research hopes to uncover concurrent developmental anomalies in the central nervous system (CNS) and the enteric nervous system (ENS). This repository explores *GDNF* genomic and epigenomic expression, and this is the start of a deep dive into how *GDNF* regulates not only the brain, but the gut. GDNF functions as a chemoattractant during embryologic development, guiding enteric neural crest cells. This binding triggers vital intracellular downstream survival and proliferation cascades, heavily utilizing the canonical Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK MAP kinase pathway.

GDNF stimulation has the potential to ameliorate symptoms of gut dysbiosis. GDNF is produced by enteric glial cells and enterocytes (gut lining cells). It binds to RET receptors and recruits junctional proteins like desmoglein 2 (DSG2) to seal cell-to-cell borders, preventing "leaky gut" and dampening inflammation.
GDNF activates RET-dependent signaling pathways (such as Src kinase) that stimulate intestinal stem cells (LGR5+) and speed up the repair of damaged tissue.

Interstitial Cells of Cajal (ICCs) serve as the primary pacemakers of the gastrointestinal tract and require intact glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) signaling via GFR$RET receptor complexes for structural maintenance and network integrity [[1]](#ref1). They operate as non-linear coupled oscillators with oscillations occurring ideally about 3 cycles per minute. In the gastrointestinal small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO), this oscillation jumps to 11-12 times per minute.

GDNF stimulation is thought to restore these disrupted pathways to the ideal physiological baseline.

### Autonomic-Enteric Coupling & Pacemaker Frequencies
Gastric slow waves operate at a baseline rhythm of approximately 3 cycles per minute ($\text{cpm}$), driven by healthy Interstitial Cell of Cajal (ICC) networks. Pathological states—such as small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) or autonomic dysregulation—disrupt this entrainment, driving aberrant high-frequency contractions (11–12 $\text{cpm}$). Because the enteric nervous system (ENS) is tightly coupled with central autonomic centers via vagal pathways, restoring neurotrophic tone via GDNF signaling may re-establish harmonious phase-locking across the heart-brain-gut axis, stabilizing baseline pacemaking frequencies.
