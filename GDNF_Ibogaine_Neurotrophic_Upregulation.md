**Subject:** Molecular Biology & Structural Neurobiology

## $\color{mediumpurple}{\text{Abstract}}$

While higher-dose ibogaine is traditionally characterized by its ability to attentuate or modulate dopaminergic circuits in addiction medicine, its capacity for Glial Cell-Derived Neurotrophic Factor ($\large{\textit{GDNF}}$) upregulation suggests a more nuanced application in development. This research proposes considering a shift from high-dose 'flood' model of isolated ibogaine toward an integrated, whole-plant iboga framework for neurotrophic repair. 

By studying models (e.g., iPSC brain organoids and computational frameworks) of pulsed, sub-perceptual or low dosing, a hormetic approach analogous to Low-Dose Naltrexone (LDN), this model seeks to maximize $\large{\textit{GDNF}}$ epigenetic expression while mitigating the $\large\textit{hERG}$ -related cardiac risks typically associated with large concentrated ibogaine alkaloid 'floods.' This research posits the benefits of this can extend beyond addiction and PTSD models to include autism spectrum disorder (ASD), which is characterized by altered neural pruning and neurogenetics. Importantly, his synthesis is intended for theoretical mapping of neurotrophic pathways (e.g., computational modeling, IRB-approved studies in animal and organoid models) and does not constitute medical advice, human experimentation desires, or a protocol for personal experimentation.

This research explores the intersection of exogenous alkaloid administration and endogenous neurotrophic factor upregulation. Specifically, the capacity of ibogaine to upregulate Glial Cell-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (GDNF) is examined as a mechanism for inducing a "device firmware upgrade" (attentuation and restoration of function) of dopaminergic circuits. It is hypothesized that a pulsed, hormetic microdosing schedule can achieve similar results to the autoregulatory $\large{\textit{GDNF}}$ loop triggered by the 40 mg/kg threshold observed in preclinical rat models [[2]](#ref2). By maintaining a consistent neurotrophic signal below the $\large{\textit{hERG}}$ inhibition ceiling, this research aims to induce a self-sustaining, long-term upregulation and efficiency maximization of the reward system (particularly within the mesolimbic reward pathway).


## $\color{mediumpurple}{\text{Introduction: The Bio-Harmonic Framework}}$

Ibogaine is an alkaloid derived from the shrub *Tabernanthe iboga*, which has been shown to upregulate $\large{\textit{GDNF}}$. This has been shown to be beneficial for systemic mental disorders such as post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and addiction [[1]](#ref1). However, it carries significant cardiac risks which cannot be understated, specifically via $\large{\textit{hERG}}$ channel inhibition, which necessitates a shift in focus toward low-dose hormetic protocols and pharmacological non-cardiotoxic analogs [[1]](#ref1). This approach suggests that a quiet, steady presence of these compounds can nurture neural repair and $\large{\textit{GDNF}}$ expression without disturbing the heart's essential harmony. Also of interest is studying the lesser-known alkaloids in the whole plant extract in theoretical models to see if some of these may attentuate the significant cardiac risks of the more extreme concentrated ibogaine (though this is not proven, and self-experimentation outside of a controlled medical setting is highly dangerous).

The neural architecture is viewed here not as a static machine, but as a dynamic, resonant system. Using principles of molecular symmetry, the stability of indole-based scaffolds can be analyzed in relation to microtubule-mediated signaling. This model prioritizes a synergistic priming phase, utilizing non-invasive modalities such as *Hericium erinaceus* biochemical supplementation, multimodal aerobic exercise, and mitochondrial photobiomodulation  (if indicated) to create a fertile landscape for neurotrophic flourishing. The larger 40 mg/kg 'flood' dose in rodent models is known to trigger a positive feedback loop where $\large{\textit{GDNF}}$ stimulates its own expression, leading to long-lasting neurological changes [[2]](#ref2). This study proposes that sub-perceptual, cumulative pulsing may reach this same transcriptional threshold through temporal summation. This would allow for a long-term transition to homeostatic equilibrium (which can be modeled as a limit cycle attractor for long-term activation versus a decaying parabolic spike for short-term activation) with lesser cardiac risks (though still significant) associated with high-dose isolated alkaloids. More pharmaceutical analogues should also be researched and developed that use the iboga whole-plant alkaloids but are significantly less cardiotoxic. Beyond ibogaine, the lesser-known iboga plant alkaloids that create a synergistic effect should be researched for analogue development.

## $\color{mediumpurple}{\text{Methodology}}$

### $\color{mediumpurple}{\text{The Recursive Dendrite Model}}$

This model posits that neurotrophic restoration follows a recursive growth algorithm. Unlike linear repair, where a stimulus leads to a fixed amount of growth, a recursive model suggests that each new dendritic branch increased by $\large{\textit{GDNF}}$ signaling serves as a new platform for further receptor expression.

### $\color{mediumpurple}{\text{Connection to Neurodivergent Phenotypes}}$

Autistic brains have shown hypopruning during adolescence. This is thought to produce a potential imbalance as the signal tries to integrate with the autistic cerebellum that has lower Purkinje cells relative to neurotypical phenotypes (high signal, low integration). This hypopruning state can be modeled by adjusting the depth factor on the model to a higher number such as 14. *GDNF* stimulation, whether through hormetic low-dose iboga alkaloid consumption or long-distance running, is thought to normalize the pruning of neurons, restoring them to an optimal depth factor that allows for efficient adult synaptic transmission and integration while preserving some neuroplasticity capacity present in juvenile states.

## $\color{mediumpurple}{\text{Implications and Future Directions}}$

This framework postulates that the benefits of pulsed, low-dose hormetic iboga may extend far beyond addiction recovery and dopamine signal normalization. Interestingly, autistic people have been shown to have lower levels of *GDNF* gene expression, and lower levels correlate with more severe behavioral and cognitive deficits [[3]](#ref3). Ibogaine also stimulates *BDNF*, and interestingly, as an autistic person, my genomic testing showed a variant that [impairs *BDNF* signaling](https://github.com/curiousquantumelephant777/systems-biology-functional-medicine/blob/main/beyond-thyroid-functional-med-insights/mthfr-bdnf-insight.md).
It is hypothesized that pulsed low levels of these alkaloids could theoretically lead to increased $GDNF$ expression through an imprinting-like topological and neurochemical mechanism. The low-dose nature of this framework could somewhat lessen the cardiac risk profile, though it still remains a serious, potentially life-threatening concern concern. To test this, we could use iPSC organoid models of ASD and compare *GDNF* expression before and after iboga alkaloid or analogue pulsed hormetic administration (using neurotypical organoids as a control group). To see if the effect persists over time, gene expression should be measured longitudinally (immediately after completing the dosing protocol, 1 month after, 3 months after, 6 months after, and 12 months after). The results of this could then be extended to animal models (e.g., rodent models).
