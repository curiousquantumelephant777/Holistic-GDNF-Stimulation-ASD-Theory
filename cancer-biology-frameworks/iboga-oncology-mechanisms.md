# 🧬 Indole Alkaloids in Oncology: Apoptosis and Cytotoxic Mechanisms

## Background
- Exploring how monoterpene indole alkaloid scaffolds interact with apoptotic pathways and cell cycle regulation in specific cancer cell models.
- In nature, organisms produce specialized toxins and noxious chemicals as an evolutionary defense mechanism to deter predators, pests, or pathogens. Examples include iboga alkaloids produced by the iboga shrub and 5-MeO-DMT produced by *Incilus alvarius* (Colorado river) toads.
- These defenses have beenrefined over millions of years to disrupt fundamental cellular processes like cell division and energy production. Cancer researchers have successfully repurposed them to target the rapid, unregulated proliferation of malignant human cells [[1]](#ref1)
    - Naturally-occuring tryptophan alkaloids (indoles) have shown significant anticancer properties. For instance, Vinca alkaloids (extracted from the Madagascar periwinkle) are used to synthesize Vincristine (a chemotherapeutic drug). These compounds prevent microtubule assembly to halt tumor proliferation [[2]](#ref2).
    - Intriguingly, to create vinblastine or vincristine, the Madagascar Periwinkle (*Catharanthus roseus*, formerly *Vinca rosea*) must chemically fuse two separate plant molecules together: vindoline and catharanthine.
Catharanthine possesses an "iboga-type" chemical skeleton. It is the exact optical mirror image (enantiomer) of (-)-coronaridine, which is the direct biological precursor to (-)-ibogaine [[3]](#ref3).

## Hypotheses
- Investigating mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization and caspase cascade activation triggered by specific alkaloid derivatives.
- It is hypothesized that certain iboga alkaloids (whether naturally-occuring or pharmacologic analogues) may induce selective apoptosis (whereas current oncologic medications induce non-selective apoptosis). By upregulating neurotrophic factors and attentuating multi-receptor systems, iboga-like motifs may help with cancer prevention or improvement of precancerous lesions.
- The defensive evolutionary nature of iboga alkaloids, at low doses, may provide a hormetic stressor.  

## Future Directions
- Mining transcriptomic datasets to cross-reference neurotrophic signaling receptor overlap with oncology targets.
- Constructing a phylogenetic tree based on sequencing using Python modules to analyze the selective apoptotic properties of iboga alkaloids from an evolutionary biological perspective.
